Q: The lecture states nine learning goals. What are the first four?
A: **Describe the structure of DNA**; **describe the function of the enzymes of replication**; **describe the anatomy of a replication fork**; **explain how accuracy in replication is achieved**.
Tags: core

---

Q+: And the last five?
A: **Explain the causes of replicative stress**; **explain how homologous recombination underpins DNA replication**; **describe the roles of important common factors in homologous recombination and the replication stress response**; **describe the main techniques used to study them**; **explain how replication stress and homologous recombination defects can be exploited for cancer treatments**.
Tags: core

---

Q: What does the lecture's opening DNA slide label, and whose figure is it?
A: The **antiparallel 3' and 5' ends**, the **34 Å** helical repeat, the **3.4 Å** rise per base pair and the **10 Å** radius, beside the **A-T and G-C** base pairs. From **Watson and Crick, *Nature* 171, 737-738 (1953)**.
Tags: structure, numbers

---

Q: Name every component labelled on the anatomy-of-a-replication-fork slide.
A: **Parental DNA helix**, **DNA helicase**, **DNA primase**, **single-strand DNA-binding protein**, **RNA primer**, **DNA polymerase on the leading strand** with its **sliding clamp**, **DNA polymerase on the lagging strand** finishing an **Okazaki fragment**, and the **leading-strand and lagging-strand templates**.
Tags: fork, core

---

Q+: The second version of that figure redraws it. What changes, and why?
A: The **lagging-strand template is looped back** so that both polymerases sit in one complex at the fork and travel in the same direction, even though the lagging strand is synthesized backwards. This is what the **"Replication fork coupling"** animation shows.
Tags: fork, core

---

Q: Which strand needs an RNA primer for every fragment, and which needs one only at the start?
A: The **lagging strand** needs a **new RNA primer for each Okazaki fragment**; the **leading strand** is primed once and then synthesized continuously.
Tags: fork, core

---

Q: List the causes of DNA replication stress from the lecture's diagram, with what deals with each.
A: **DNA lesions** (repair, DNA damage tolerance); **ribonucleotide incorporation** (RNase H); **DNA secondary structure** (helicases such as BLM and WRN); **repetitive DNA** (helicases); **transcription and RNA-DNA hybrids** (RNA processing machinery, helicases, topoisomerases, RNase H); **limiting nucleotides** (regulation of origin firing); and **fragile sites or oncogene-induced stress**.
Tags: stress, core

---

Q: On the consequences slide, what three things cause replication stress?
A: **Shortage of replication factors or dNTPs**; **oncogenic stress**, for example **MYC or RAS**, and transcriptome collision; and **DNA-bound obstacles**, which are DNA-bound proteins, DNA lesions and DNA secondary structures.
Tags: stress, core

---

Q+: What happens at the fork itself, and which sensor is activated?
A: The **polymerase is blocked while the MCM helicase keeps going**, leaving **single-stranded DNA coated by RPA**. On that RPA filament **ATR with ATRIP** is recruited and activated with **TopBP1**, and **ATR phosphorylates CHK1**, with **DNA-PK** and **WEE1** feeding in.
Tags: stress, core

---

Q+: What are the five outputs of the replication stress response?
A: **S-phase checkpoint activation**; **control of new origin firing**; **replication fork stabilisation and restart**; **prevention of fork collapse**; and **DNA repair**.
Tags: stress, core

---

Q: What turns a stalled fork into a double-strand break?
A: **Collapse.** If the response fails, **endonucleases** act on the collapsed fork and produce a **DNA double-strand break**.
Tags: stress, core

---

Q: The lecture plays two animations for repair in S phase. What is the first, and what is special about the break?
A: **Homologous recombination repair of a two-ended double-strand break by synthesis-dependent strand annealing (SDSA)**. Both ends are present, so after resection one end invades, is extended, and is annealed back to the other end.
Tags: hr, core

---

Q+: And the second?
A: **Restoration of a broken replication fork by homologous recombination**: a **one-ended** break, where the invading end re-establishes a replication fork rather than annealing back. This is why homologous recombination is part of normal replication, not just a repair pathway.
Tags: hr, core

---

Q: Put the factors of homologous recombination in the order the flow diagram gives them.
A: **DSB**; **DSB binding by MRN (MRE11-RAD50-NBS1)**; **end resection by EXO1 and BLM-DNA2**; **RPA binding and ATR-ATRIP activation**; **RAD51 loading by BRCA1-PALB2-BRCA2**; **invasion and D-loop extension by a DNA polymerase**; **double Holliday junction intermediate**; **resolution by resolvases**.
Tags: hr, core

---

Q+: Which step does the lecturer circle in red, and why does it matter?
A: **RAD51 loading by BRCA1, PALB2 and BRCA2.** It is the committed step, and it is where the BRCA proteins do their work in this pathway.
Tags: hr, core

---

Q: What three statements does the lecture make about the BRCA proteins?
A: **BRCA1/2 mutations promote tumor growth**; **BRCA1/2 deletion in primary cells inhibits growth**; and **loss of homologous recombination is believed to be the major catalyst for both**.
Tags: brca, core

---

Q: BRCA1/2 have a second job besides homologous recombination. What is it?
A: They **protect replication forks from nuclease-mediated degradation**. Without them, **MRE11** attacks the reversed fork and **degrades the nascent strands**.
Tags: brca, core

---

Q+: What are the two consequences of that degradation on the slide?
A: **Loss of viability** and **chemotherapy sensitivity**.
Tags: brca, core

---

Q: Draw the lecture's BRCA1/2 diagram in words.
A: **BRCA1/2** sits above two arms, **homologous recombination** and **replication fork stability**, which meet again at **viability and genome stability**. When BRCA1/2 is lost, the same diagram reads **cell death** and **genome instability**. References: **Moynahan et al., Jasin lab, *Mol. Cell* 1999** and **Schlacher et al., Jasin lab, *Cell* 2011**.
Tags: brca, core

Q: The techniques slide is a tree with a mouse at the top. What are its three branches?
A: **Electron microscopy**, **cell biology**, and **biochemistry and proteomics**.
Tags: techniques, core

---

Q+: What does each branch give you?
A: **Electron microscopy**: structural visualization of replication intermediates. **Cell biology**: single-molecule analysis of DNA replication, and immunofluorescence analysis of replication proteins. **Biochemistry and proteomics**: analysis of protein dynamics at replication forks.
Tags: techniques, core

---

Q: How are fragile replication intermediates preserved for electron microscopy?
A: With **trimethyl psoralen and 366 nm light**, which **crosslinks the strands in vivo** and stabilizes the structure that was actually there before spreading it for EM. Protocol from **J. Sogo, ETH Zürich**; **Zellweger and Lopes, *Meth. Mol. Biol.* 2017**.
Tags: techniques, core

---

Q: What does a RAD51 immunofluorescence experiment show in untreated versus stressed cells?
A: **Untreated nuclei are almost blank; stressed nuclei are full of bright RAD51 foci.** A focus is the microscopic shadow of a nucleoprotein filament at a site of damage.
Tags: techniques, core

---

Q+: And how is the same assay used to test homologous recombination competence?
A: After **ionizing radiation**, **wild-type cells form abundant RAD51 foci** while **BRCA1/2-deficient cells form none**: the damage is there, but RAD51 never gets loaded.
Tags: techniques, core

---

Q: In the gene targeting experiment, what are the numbers, and what do they prove?
A: **Wild type: 192/192 clones targeted.** **Brca2-null with shPtip1: 0/96. With shPtip2: 0/96.** Gene targeting depends completely on homologous recombination, and knocking down PTIP does **not** restore it, even though PTIP knockdown restores fork protection. The two BRCA functions are separable.
Tags: techniques, brca, core

---

Q: How does the DR-GFP reporter assay work?
A: The cassette carries **SceGFP**, a GFP interrupted by an **I-SceI site**, and **iGFP**, a promoterless internal fragment. Expressing **I-SceI** from **pCMV3xNLS-I-SceI** makes **one defined double-strand break**; if the cell repairs it by **homologous recombination** using iGFP as template, the cell becomes **green**. Cells are scored **with and without I-SceI**.
Tags: techniques, core

---

Q: Define synthetic lethality using the lecture's grid.
A: Losing **gene A alone** leaves the cell **alive**; losing **gene B alone** leaves it **alive**; losing **both together** kills it.
Tags: parp, core

---

Q: Draw the domain organization of PARP1.
A: A **DNA-binding domain** (ZF1, ZF2, NLS, ZF3), an **automodification domain** (BRCT), and a **catalytic domain** (WGR, CD).
Tags: parp, core

---

Q+: What does PARP1 do once it recognizes damage, and what is consumed?
A: It builds **mono(ADP-ribose)** and **poly(ADP-ribose) chains**, consuming **NAD⁺** and releasing **nicotinamide**. The chains are removed again by **PARG, ARH3 and OARD1**.
Tags: parp, core

---

Q+: What is the PAR signal used for, on the five boxes of that slide?
A: **Repair of ssDNA nicks and breaks**; **repair of bulky lesions**; **repair of double-strand breaks**; **stabilization of replication forks**; and **chromatin modifications**.
Tags: parp, core

---

Q: Name the PARP inhibitors on the lecture's slide with their codes.
A: **Olaparib (Lynparza), AZD-2281**; **veliparib, ABT-888**; **niraparib, MK-4827**; **rucaparib, AG-014699**; **talazoparib, BMN-673**; and **E7016**. All are drawn around the same core, highlighted in red.
Tags: parp

Q: Give the mechanism of synthetic lethality between BRCA and PARP deficiency, following the slide's branches.
A: A **single-strand lesion at a replication fork** is normally repaired by **PARP-dependent SSB/BER repair**, and **replication continues**. With a **PARP inhibitor**, the lesion persists, the fork **collapses and a double-strand break is induced**. With **normal HR** that break is repaired and replication continues; with **defective HR**, damage accumulates and the **cell dies**.
Tags: parp, core

---

Q+: When the lecturer asks "HOW? DNA damage?", which experiment answers it?
A: **Metaphase spread analysis**, scoring **breaks, gaps, quadriradials** and complex damage: the killing really does run through unrepaired chromosomal damage.
Tags: parp, techniques

A: A **BRCA1/2 mutation** appears; **tumor growth** follows; and **PARPi or cisplatin treatment** kills the tumour cells selectively. The section is titled **"Homologous recombination and replication stress in health and tumorigenesis"**.
